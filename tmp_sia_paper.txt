Poblacion, Kidapawan City. Founded on March 25, 1997 by the couple Ritche and Colette Mearns, the business has served the Kidapawan, North Cotabato community for decades. It operates Wednesday to Monday from 10:00 am to 9:00 pm. Organizational Chart/Structure:     /Business Process:  Before taking orders, the server checks with the kitchen to confirm which menu items are not available. Once confirmed, the customer selects items from the menu, and the server writes the order on the guest check. The original guest check is then given to the kitchen for food preparation, while a duplicate copy is provided to the cashier for billing purposes. If an ordered item is found to be unavailable, the server returns to the customer to inform them and ask whether they would like to replace the item or cancel it. When the food is ready, the expeditor rings the bell and tells the server which table the order is for. The server then delivers the food to the appropriate table. After the meal, the server provides the bill to the customer for payment. Costing and pricing are maintained on paper. Ingredient AP prices are updated informally and yields are computed per recipe by hand. Purchasing is simplified—staff request items and the Owner buys directly without POs; invoices are kept for reference. Inventory movements (stock-in, transfers to shelf, kitchen usage, spoilage, and counts) are not systematically recorded, which leads to visibility gaps, price drift, and variance issues.   Identified Problems:  	The identified problem observed is the costing database for each recipe is in pen and paper form which is hard to manage and can be misplaced. New recipes are manually solved. The prices of ingredients are also constantly changing and there is also a need for change in prices in the menu. The second problem is there is no record for the inventory stock in and stock out. No standardized AP price history/audit → menu prices lag true cost. Purchasing is ad-hoc (no POs) → partial fills and supplier changes aren’t tracked. No formal POS sync for price changes → risk of mismatched in-store prices. 2.0 Events Table, Use Case List, Use Case Diagram  2.1Events Table  1.1Costing of Portions  Cost/Portion (B) = (Batch Cost ÷ Total Yield) × Portion Size  Price = Cost ÷ FC%  Tip: Always write AP with units, e.g., ₱/kg or ₱/L, to avoid confusion. / Single Ingredient Example (Carrots) Item: Carrots  Qty Used (B): 100  Unit for B: g  AP Cost: ₱100 per kg (₱/kg)  Yield %: 80%  EP Unit Cost = AP ÷ Yield% = ₱100/kg ÷ 0.80 = ₱125/kg = ₱0.125/g  Line Cost (Carrots) = 100 g × ₱0.125/g = ₱12.50 / Small Recipe Example (4 items → Batch Cost → Cost/Portion → Price) Assumed Total Yield (cooked): 1,000 g  Portion Size: 250 g Item Qt y Uni t AP Cost Yiel d EP Unit Cost Line Cost Carrots 100 g ₱100/kg 80% 100 ÷ 0.80 = ₱125/kg (₱0.125/g) 100×0.125 = ₱12.50 Onions 50 g ₱80/kg 90% 80 ÷ 0.90 = ₱88.89/kg (₱0.08889/g) 50×0.08889 ≈ ₱4.44 Butter 20 g ₱250/kg 100% ₱250/kg (₱0.25/g) 20×0.25 = ₱5.00 Stock 500 mL ₱60/L (₱0.06/mL) 100% ₱60/L (₱0.06/mL) 500×0.06 = ₱30.00 Batch Cost (₱) = 12.50 + 4.44 + 5.00 + 30.00 = ₱51.94 Number of Portions = Total Yield ÷ Portion Size = 1,000 g ÷ 250 g = 4 portions Cost/Portion (A) = Batch Cost ÷ #
